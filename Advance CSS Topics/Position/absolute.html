The national flag of Bangladesh was officially adopted on January 17, 1972. It features a red circle symbolizing the blood of those who died in the 1971 Liberation War, set slightly to the hoist side on a green background that represents the lushness of the country and the vitality of its people.

The original version of the flag used during the Liberation War also had a yellow map of Bangladesh inside the red circle, but it was later removed to simplify the design. The flag stands as a symbol of the nation’s hard-fought independence and enduring pride.

The green field of the flag signifies the eternal youth and freshness of the land of Bangladesh. The red circle is slightly off-center to ensure it appears centered when the flag is flying. It also serves as a reminder of the sun rising over Bengal.

The flag design was first hoisted at Dhaka University on March 2, 1971, by student leaders who later played a vital role in the independence movement. Today, the flag continues to inspire unity, resilience, and love for the nation.
